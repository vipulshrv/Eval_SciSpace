# Comparative Efficacy and Safety of GLP-1 Receptor Agonists for Weight Loss: Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, and Liraglutide

## Executive Summary

This report provides a comprehensive comparison of three glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists—semaglutide, tirzepatide, and liraglutide—for the treatment of obesity and overweight. Based on systematic analysis of Phase 3 randomized controlled trials, meta-analyses, and cardiovascular outcome trials, tirzepatide demonstrates superior weight loss efficacy (15.0–20.9% body weight reduction at 72 weeks), followed by semaglutide (14.9–16.0% at 68 weeks), and liraglutide (approximately 8% at 56 weeks). All three agents show favorable cardiovascular safety profiles, with semaglutide demonstrating a 26% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in high-risk populations. Gastrointestinal adverse events are common across all agents but generally mild-to-moderate and transient. Tirzepatide, as a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist, represents the most potent weight loss therapy currently available, though with slightly higher discontinuation rates at the highest dose. These findings support the use of GLP-1 receptor agonists as effective pharmacotherapy for obesity management with acceptable safety profiles.

## 1. Introduction

Obesity represents a global health crisis affecting over 650 million adults worldwide, contributing substantially to cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and premature mortality. Traditional lifestyle interventions achieve modest weight loss (3–5%), often insufficient to reverse obesity-related complications. Pharmacotherapy has emerged as a critical adjunct to lifestyle modification, with GLP-1 receptor agonists demonstrating unprecedented efficacy in weight reduction.

This report systematically compares three GLP-1 receptor agonists approved or investigated for weight management: semaglutide (Wegovy®, 2.4 mg subcutaneous weekly), tirzepatide (Zepbound®, 5–15 mg subcutaneous weekly), and liraglutide (Saxenda®, 3.0 mg subcutaneous daily). We evaluate their comparative efficacy in percent body weight reduction, cardiovascular outcomes, and adverse event profiles to inform clinical decision-making.

## 2. Background and Mechanism of Action

### 2.1 GLP-1 Receptor Agonist Pharmacology

GLP-1 receptor agonists mimic the action of endogenous glucagon-like peptide-1, an incretin hormone secreted by intestinal L-cells in response to nutrient intake. These agents promote weight loss through multiple mechanisms: enhanced satiety via hypothalamic appetite centers, delayed gastric emptying, reduced food intake, and improved glucose-dependent insulin secretion [11].

### 2.2 Tirzepatide: A Dual Incretin Agonist

Tirzepatide represents a novel pharmacological class as a dual glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and GLP-1 receptor agonist. This dual mechanism enhances weight loss efficacy beyond selective GLP-1 agonism, with GIP contributing to improved insulin sensitivity, enhanced lipolysis, and potentially greater central appetite suppression [21], [22].

### 2.3 Comparative Pharmacokinetics

Semaglutide and tirzepatide are administered once weekly, offering superior adherence compared to liraglutide's daily dosing. Semaglutide has a half-life of approximately 1 week, while tirzepatide has a half-life of approximately 5 days. Liraglutide, with a 13-hour half-life, requires daily administration [19].

## 3. Methods and Evidence Base

This comparative analysis synthesizes evidence from three comprehensive literature searches encompassing:

- **Semaglutide**: 74 papers including STEP 1–5 trials, STEP-HFpEF, SELECT trial, and multiple systematic reviews and meta-analyses
- **Tirzepatide**: 26 papers including SURMOUNT-1 to -4 trials, SURPASS trials, and cardiovascular safety analyses
- **Liraglutide**: 64 papers including SCALE trials, LEADER cardiovascular outcomes trial, and safety studies

Evidence was prioritized from Phase 3 randomized controlled trials (RCTs), systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and cardiovascular outcome trials. Weight loss outcomes are reported as mean percent body weight reduction from baseline, with proportions achieving clinically meaningful thresholds (≥5%, ≥10%, ≥15% weight loss). Cardiovascular outcomes focus on MACE (composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, and nonfatal stroke) and heart failure events. Adverse events emphasize gastrointestinal tolerability and discontinuation rates.

## 4. Weight Loss Efficacy: Comparative Analysis

### 4.1 Semaglutide Weight Loss Outcomes

Semaglutide 2.4 mg once weekly demonstrates robust and sustained weight loss across the STEP clinical trial program. In STEP 1, participants without type 2 diabetes achieved a mean body weight reduction of **14.9%** over 68 weeks, with 86.4% achieving ≥5%, 69.1% achieving ≥10%, and 50.5% achieving ≥15% weight loss [5]. STEP 3, which combined semaglutide with intensive behavioral therapy, reported even greater efficacy with **16.0%** mean weight reduction (95% CI, -12.0 to -8.6), with 86.6% achieving ≥5%, 75.3% achieving ≥10%, and 55.8% achieving ≥15% weight loss [4].

Long-term efficacy was confirmed in STEP 5, where 104 weeks of treatment resulted in **15.2%** mean body weight reduction (95% CI, -15.3 to -9.8), with 77.1% achieving ≥5%, 61.8% achieving ≥10%, and 52.1% achieving ≥15% weight loss [2]. A meta-analysis of three RCTs (n=2,783) reported a weighted mean difference of **-12.0%** (95% CI -13.9%, -10.2%) at 68 weeks, with 86.5% of participants achieving ≥5% weight loss compared to 34.8% with placebo [8].

In the SELECT trial, which enrolled participants with preexisting cardiovascular disease, semaglutide achieved a mean weight reduction of **10.2%** sustained over 208 weeks (4 years), demonstrating durability of effect [15].

### 4.2 Tirzepatide Weight Loss Outcomes

Tirzepatide demonstrates dose-dependent weight loss superior to semaglutide. In the pivotal SURMOUNT-1 trial (72 weeks), tirzepatide achieved mean body weight reductions of **15.0%** (5 mg), **19.5%** (10 mg), and **20.9%** (15 mg), compared to **3.1%** with placebo [22], [26]. The proportion achieving ≥5% weight loss was 85% (5 mg), 89% (10 mg), and 91% (15 mg). Notably, 48% (5 mg), 67% (10 mg), and 71% (15 mg) achieved ≥20% weight loss, a threshold rarely reached with other pharmacotherapies [22].

A systematic review and meta-analysis including SURMOUNT-2 reported dose-dependent weight loss: **-8.07%** (5 mg), **-10.79%** (10 mg), and **-11.83%** (15 mg) over 12–72 weeks [27]. In SURMOUNT-2, which enrolled participants with type 2 diabetes and obesity, tirzepatide 10 mg and 15 mg achieved **12.8%** and **14.7%** mean weight reduction, respectively [32].

Real-world evidence from routine clinical practice demonstrated tirzepatide achieved **-16.3%** (95% CI, -16.7 to -15.9) mean weight reduction over 12 months, exceeding semaglutide in head-to-head comparison [30].

### 4.3 Liraglutide Weight Loss Outcomes

Liraglutide 3.0 mg daily demonstrates more modest but clinically meaningful weight loss. In the SCALE trials, liraglutide achieved a mean weight loss of **8.4 ± 7.3 kg** compared to **2.8 ± 6.5 kg** with placebo over 56 weeks, corresponding to approximately **8.0 ± 6.7%** body weight reduction [1], [2]. The proportion achieving ≥5% weight loss was 63.2% with liraglutide versus 27.1% with placebo [1].

The SCALE Diabetes trial, which enrolled participants with type 2 diabetes, reported similar efficacy with liraglutide 3.0 mg [12]. Long-term real-world data confirmed sustained weight loss over 12 months in clinical practice settings [20].

## 5. Cardiovascular Outcomes

### 5.1 Semaglutide Cardiovascular Outcomes

Semaglutide demonstrates significant cardiovascular benefits in high-risk populations. In the SUSTAIN 6 trial, subcutaneous semaglutide (0.5 mg or 1.0 mg weekly) reduced the risk of MACE by **26%** (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.58–0.95; p=0.02 for superiority), primarily driven by a reduction in nonfatal stroke [5], [7]. The SELECT trial, which enrolled 17,604 participants with preexisting cardiovascular disease, overweight or obesity, and without diabetes, demonstrated that semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced MACE by **20%** over a median follow-up of 40 months [15].

A meta-analysis reported that semaglutide reduced composite major cardiovascular events by **14%** (HR 0.86; 95% CI 0.80–0.93), myocardial infarction by **13%** (HR 0.87; 95% CI 0.77–0.98), stroke by **18%** (HR 0.82; 95% CI 0.73–0.92), and death from cardiovascular disease by **13%** (HR 0.87; 95% CI 0.81–0.94) [14].

In the STEP-HFpEF trial, semaglutide improved symptoms, physical function, and exercise capacity in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) and obesity, with benefits proportional to the magnitude of weight loss [3].

### 5.2 Tirzepatide Cardiovascular Outcomes

Tirzepatide demonstrates cardiovascular safety with emerging evidence of benefit. A prespecified meta-analysis of the SURPASS trials in type 2 diabetes reported hazard ratios of **0.8** (95% CI 0.57–1.11) for MACE-4, **0.9** (95% CI 0.50–1.61) for cardiovascular death, **0.76** (95% CI 0.45–1.28) for myocardial infarction, and **0.81** (95% CI 0.39–1.68) for ischemic stroke [31]. For MACE-6 (including heart failure events), the HR was **0.79** (95% CI 0.58–1.06) [31].

Post-hoc analyses of SURMOUNT-1 demonstrated that tirzepatide significantly reduced the 10-year predicted risk of developing atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD). At week 72, the relative change in ASCVD risk from baseline was **-16.4%**, **-23.5%**, and **-22.4%** for tirzepatide 5, 10, and 15 mg, respectively, compared to **12.7%** for placebo [24].

In the SUMMIT trial, tirzepatide demonstrated symptomatic and functional improvements in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and obesity, with fewer worsening heart failure events versus placebo [25]. The ongoing SURPASS-CVOT trial will provide definitive cardiovascular outcomes data in patients with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease [27].

### 5.3 Liraglutide Cardiovascular Outcomes

Liraglutide 1.8 mg (approved for type 2 diabetes) demonstrated cardiovascular benefit in the LEADER trial. The primary composite outcome (cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke) occurred at a significantly lower rate with liraglutide (HR 0.87; 95% CI 0.78–0.97), with significant reductions in cardiovascular mortality (4.7% vs 6.0%) and all-cause mortality (8.2% vs 9.6%) over 3.8 years [1], [15].

For liraglutide 3.0 mg (weight management dose), a post-hoc analysis of SCALE trials reported the primary composite cardiovascular outcome occurred in 8 participants (1.54 events/1000 person-years) versus 10 participants (3.65 events/1000 person-years) in comparators, yielding a HR of **0.42** (95% CI 0.17–1.08), though with wide confidence intervals due to the low event rate [16].

### 5.4 Comparative Summary: Cardiovascular Outcomes

All three GLP-1 receptor agonists demonstrate cardiovascular safety, with semaglutide and liraglutide showing established cardiovascular benefit in high-risk populations. Semaglutide's 20–26% MACE reduction in SELECT and SUSTAIN 6 represents robust evidence of cardiovascular protection. Tirzepatide shows promising cardiovascular safety signals, with ongoing trials expected to confirm benefit. Liraglutide's cardiovascular benefit is well-established at the 1.8 mg diabetes dose, with emerging evidence at the 3.0 mg weight management dose.

## 6. Adverse Events and Safety Profile

### 6.1 Semaglutide Adverse Events

Gastrointestinal adverse events are the most common side effects of semaglutide, occurring in **82–83%** of participants in STEP trials. In STEP 5, nausea occurred in **53.3%**, diarrhea in **34.9%**, and vomiting in **30.3%** of participants [2]. In STEP 3, gastrointestinal disorders affected **82.8%**, with nausea in **58.2%**, constipation in **36.9%**, and diarrhea in **36.1%** [4].

These events are typically mild-to-moderate in severity, transient, and occur predominantly during dose escalation. Discontinuation due to adverse events ranged from **5.9%** to **7.0%** across STEP trials, with **3.9–4.3%** discontinuing specifically due to gastrointestinal disorders [2], [5]. Serious adverse events occurred in **7.9–9.8%** of participants [2], [4].

Notable safety concerns include gallbladder-related disorders (**2.6–4.9%**), with cholelithiasis risk increased at **1.4%** [2], [10]. A meta-analysis reported increased risk of gastrointestinal adverse events (RR 3.26; 95% CI 1.99–5.34) and treatment discontinuation (RR 2.03; 95% CI 1.87–2.20), but no significant difference in serious adverse events (RR 1.07; 95% CI 0.70–1.62) [16].

In SUSTAIN 6, semaglutide was associated with increased risk of diabetic retinopathy complications (HR 1.76; p=0.02), though most events occurred early in treatment [10]. Semaglutide carries a boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors based on rodent studies, contraindicating use in patients with personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 [10].

### 6.2 Tirzepatide Adverse Events

Tirzepatide's adverse event profile is qualitatively similar to semaglutide, with gastrointestinal events predominating. In SURMOUNT-1, nausea occurred in **24.6–33.3%**, diarrhea in **18.7–23.0%**, and vomiting in **8.3–12.2%** of participants receiving tirzepatide 5–15 mg [26]. These rates are numerically lower than semaglutide, though direct head-to-head comparisons are limited.

Discontinuation rates due to adverse events were dose-dependent: **5.1%** (5 mg), **9.1%** (10 mg), and **25%** (15 mg) in one analysis [31]. However, other SURMOUNT trials reported lower discontinuation rates of **4.6–7.3%** across doses [23], [27]. Serious adverse events occurred in **5–7%** of participants [22], [31].

A comprehensive analysis of SURMOUNT-1 to -4 trials found that **27.8–72.8%** of tirzepatide-treated participants reported gastrointestinal adverse events (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, dyspepsia) compared to **12.2–32.5%** with placebo. Most were non-serious and occurred during dose escalation. Discontinuation due to gastrointestinal adverse events ranged from **1.0% to 10.5%** [28].

Importantly, mediation analyses demonstrated that gastrointestinal adverse events contributed only **up to 3.1%** of total weight reduction with tirzepatide, indicating that weight loss is primarily driven by pharmacological mechanisms rather than adverse event-related food aversion [28].

Hypoglycemia risk was low (**<10%** in most studies), with no severe hypoglycemic events reported in participants without diabetes [22], [31]. Pancreatitis and cholecystitis were rarely encountered [31].

### 6.3 Liraglutide Adverse Events

Liraglutide 3.0 mg demonstrates a gastrointestinal adverse event profile consistent with the GLP-1 receptor agonist class. In SCALE trials, the main side effects included nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea, with a **5%** discontinuation rate due to adverse events compared to **2.7%** with placebo [1].

Specific gastrointestinal event rates in one analysis included nausea (**39.3%**), diarrhea (**20.9%**), and vomiting (**15.7%**) with liraglutide 3.0 mg, compared to **13.8%**, **9.9%**, and **6.2%** with placebo, respectively [13]. These events typically resolved after 2–3 months of treatment [13].

Discontinuation due to adverse events was **4.3%** in pooled analyses [13]. Serious adverse events included more injection site reactions (p=0.002) and acute gallstone disease (p<0.001) with liraglutide compared to placebo [13]. Neoplasms were numerically higher but not statistically significant [13].

Liraglutide is contraindicated in pregnant patients and those with a history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 2, consistent with the class warning [1].

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 Weight Loss Efficacy Hierarchy

This comparative analysis establishes a clear efficacy hierarchy: tirzepatide > semaglutide > liraglutide. Tirzepatide 15 mg achieves approximately **20.9%** mean body weight reduction at 72 weeks, representing a **5-percentage-point advantage** over semaglutide 2.4 mg (14.9–16.0%) and a **12-percentage-point advantage** over liraglutide 3.0 mg (~8%). This translates to clinically meaningful differences in the proportion of patients achieving substantial weight loss thresholds.

The superior efficacy of tirzepatide likely reflects its dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonism. GIP contributes to enhanced insulin sensitivity, increased energy expenditure, and potentially greater central appetite suppression beyond GLP-1 effects alone [21], [22]. The dose-response relationship observed with tirzepatide (15 mg > 10 mg > 5 mg) suggests that higher receptor occupancy drives greater weight loss, though at the cost of increased gastrointestinal adverse events.

Semaglutide's intermediate efficacy reflects potent and selective GLP-1 receptor agonism with optimized pharmacokinetics enabling once-weekly dosing. The consistency of weight loss across STEP 1, 3, and 5 trials (14.9–16.0%) demonstrates reproducibility and durability of effect. Notably, semaglutide combined with intensive behavioral therapy (STEP 3) achieved 16.0% weight loss, suggesting additive benefits of pharmacotherapy and lifestyle intervention [4].

Liraglutide's more modest efficacy (~8% weight loss) reflects both its selective GLP-1 agonism and shorter half-life necessitating daily dosing. While less potent than newer agents, liraglutide represents an important option with established long-term safety data and cardiovascular benefit.

### 7.2 Cardiovascular Protection: A Class Effect?

All three GLP-1 receptor agonists demonstrate cardiovascular safety, with semaglutide and liraglutide showing established cardiovascular benefit. Semaglutide's 20–26% MACE reduction in SELECT and SUSTAIN 6 trials represents robust evidence of cardiovascular protection extending beyond glycemic control [5], [14], [15]. The SELECT trial is particularly notable for demonstrating benefit in patients without diabetes, suggesting that cardiovascular protection is mediated by weight loss, blood pressure reduction, and anti-inflammatory effects rather than glucose lowering alone.

Liraglutide's cardiovascular benefit in LEADER (13% MACE reduction, HR 0.87) is well-established, with significant reductions in cardiovascular and all-cause mortality [1], [15]. The mechanisms likely include improved endothelial function, reduced arterial stiffness, decreased systemic inflammation, and favorable effects on blood pressure and lipids.

Tirzepatide's cardiovascular profile is evolving. While prespecified meta-analyses show hazard ratios favoring tirzepatide for MACE (HR 0.8), myocardial infarction (HR 0.76), and stroke (HR 0.81), confidence intervals cross 1.0, precluding definitive conclusions [31]. Post-hoc analyses demonstrating 16–24% reductions in predicted 10-year ASCVD risk are encouraging [24]. The ongoing SURPASS-CVOT trial will provide definitive evidence of cardiovascular benefit in high-risk populations.

The STEP-HFpEF and SUMMIT trials demonstrate that both semaglutide and tirzepatide improve symptoms, physical function, and exercise capacity in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and obesity, with benefits proportional to weight loss magnitude [3], [25]. This represents a novel therapeutic indication for GLP-1 receptor agonists beyond traditional cardiometabolic disease.

### 7.3 Tolerability and Adherence Considerations

Gastrointestinal adverse events represent the primary tolerability challenge for all GLP-1 receptor agonists. Semaglutide demonstrates the highest rates (82–83% any GI event, 53–58% nausea), followed by liraglutide (39% nausea), and tirzepatide (25–33% nausea) [2], [4], [13], [26]. However, these events are typically mild-to-moderate, transient, and occur predominantly during dose escalation.

Discontinuation rates due to adverse events are relatively low across all agents (4.3–7.0%), suggesting acceptable tolerability despite high GI event rates [2], [5], [13], [27]. The critical finding from tirzepatide analyses is that gastrointestinal adverse events contribute only 3.1% of total weight reduction, indicating that weight loss is primarily pharmacologically mediated rather than driven by adverse event-related food aversion [28].

Once-weekly dosing with semaglutide and tirzepatide offers adherence advantages over liraglutide's daily dosing. Real-world persistence data will be critical to understanding comparative effectiveness in clinical practice, where adherence challenges are magnified compared to controlled trial settings.

### 7.4 Clinical Phenotype Considerations

Emerging evidence suggests that patient phenotype may influence treatment response. Semaglutide demonstrates consistent efficacy across obesity classes, baseline BMI, sex, and age [6]. However, greater weight loss is observed in females and those with lower baseline body weight [6]. Tirzepatide similarly shows consistent efficacy across demographic subgroups, with dose-dependent effects across all obesity categories [26].

For patients with type 2 diabetes and obesity, both semaglutide and tirzepatide offer dual benefits of glycemic control and weight loss. STEP 2 (semaglutide in T2D) achieved 9.6% weight loss, while SURMOUNT-2 (tirzepatide in T2D) achieved 12.8–14.7% weight loss [5], [32]. Tirzepatide may offer advantages in this population given superior glycemic efficacy demonstrated in SURPASS trials.

For patients with established cardiovascular disease, semaglutide has the strongest evidence base from SELECT, demonstrating 20% MACE reduction in patients with obesity and preexisting CVD [15]. For patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and obesity, both semaglutide (STEP-HFpEF) and tirzepatide (SUMMIT) demonstrate symptomatic and functional benefits [3], [25].

### 7.5 Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

Several limitations warrant consideration. First, direct head-to-head trials comparing all three agents are limited. The STEP 8 trial compared semaglutide 2.4 mg to liraglutide 3.0 mg, demonstrating superior weight loss with semaglutide (15.8% vs 6.4%) [9], [18]. Real-world data suggest tirzepatide achieves greater weight loss than semaglutide (-16.3% vs approximately -14%) [30], but randomized controlled trial data are needed.

Second, long-term safety data beyond 2–3 years are limited for semaglutide and tirzepatide. Liraglutide has the longest safety track record, with LEADER providing 3.8 years of follow-up [1]. The durability of weight loss and cardiovascular benefit beyond trial durations remains uncertain, particularly given high rates of weight regain upon discontinuation [17].

Third, cost-effectiveness analyses are needed to inform formulary decisions and access policies. The superior efficacy of tirzepatide must be weighed against higher acquisition costs and potentially higher discontinuation rates at the 15 mg dose. Real-world effectiveness studies accounting for adherence, persistence, and healthcare utilization are critical.

Fourth, the mechanisms underlying individual variability in treatment response remain incompletely understood. Predictive biomarkers or clinical characteristics that identify optimal responders to each agent would enable personalized treatment selection.

## 8. Clinical Implications and Recommendations

### 8.1 Treatment Selection Algorithm

Based on the evidence reviewed, we propose the following treatment selection considerations:

**First-Line Considerations:**
- **Tirzepatide 10–15 mg** for patients prioritizing maximal weight loss efficacy, particularly those with BMI ≥35 kg/m² or obesity-related complications requiring substantial weight reduction
- **Semaglutide 2.4 mg** for patients with established cardiovascular disease seeking proven cardiovascular benefit alongside weight loss
- **Liraglutide 3.0 mg** for patients preferring an agent with the longest safety track record, or when once-weekly options are contraindicated or not tolerated

**Special Populations:**
- **Type 2 diabetes + obesity**: Tirzepatide or semaglutide offer superior dual benefits; tirzepatide may have slight glycemic advantage
- **Cardiovascular disease + obesity**: Semaglutide 2.4 mg has strongest evidence from SELECT trial
- **HFpEF + obesity**: Semaglutide or tirzepatide demonstrate symptomatic and functional benefits
- **Gastrointestinal intolerance**: Consider slower dose titration, antiemetic prophylaxis, or trial of alternative agent; tirzepatide may have lower nausea rates

### 8.2 Dosing and Titration Strategies

All three agents require gradual dose escalation to mitigate gastrointestinal adverse events:

- **Semaglutide**: Initiate 0.25 mg weekly × 4 weeks, then 0.5 mg × 4 weeks, then 1.0 mg × 4 weeks, then 1.7 mg × 4 weeks, then maintenance 2.4 mg weekly
- **Tirzepatide**: Initiate 2.5 mg weekly × 4 weeks, then increase by 2.5 mg increments every 4 weeks to target dose (10 or 15 mg)
- **Liraglutide**: Initiate 0.6 mg daily, increase by 0.6 mg weekly to maintenance 3.0 mg daily

Slower titration schedules may improve tolerability in patients with gastrointestinal sensitivity. Antiemetic prophylaxis (e.g., ondansetron) during dose escalation may reduce nausea-related discontinuation, though evidence is limited.

### 8.3 Monitoring and Safety Surveillance

Recommended monitoring includes:
- **Baseline**: Comprehensive metabolic panel, lipid panel, HbA1c (if diabetic), thyroid function, pregnancy test (if applicable)
- **Follow-up**: Weight, blood pressure, heart rate, gastrointestinal symptoms at each dose escalation and quarterly thereafter
- **Annual**: Comprehensive metabolic panel, lipid panel, HbA1c (if diabetic)
- **As indicated**: Gallbladder imaging if symptomatic cholelithiasis suspected; ophthalmologic examination if diabetic retinopathy concerns

Patients should be counseled on gastrointestinal adverse events, expected time course (peak during dose escalation, resolution over 2–3 months), and strategies to mitigate symptoms (smaller, more frequent meals; avoidance of high-fat foods; adequate hydration).

### 8.4 Combination and Sequential Therapy

Emerging evidence suggests potential roles for combination or sequential therapy. The STEP 1 extension demonstrated substantial weight regain upon semaglutide discontinuation, highlighting the chronic nature of obesity and need for long-term pharmacotherapy [17]. For patients with inadequate response to one agent, switching to a more potent agent (e.g., liraglutide → semaglutide → tirzepatide) represents a rational strategy, though evidence is limited.

Combination therapy with complementary mechanisms (e.g., GLP-1 RA + amylin analog, GLP-1 RA + SGLT2 inhibitor) is under investigation. The SURMOUNT-3 trial demonstrated that adding tirzepatide after intensive lifestyle intervention achieved additional 18.4% weight loss, suggesting additive benefits of pharmacotherapy and behavioral intervention [29].

## 9. Conclusion

GLP-1 receptor agonists represent a transformative advance in obesity pharmacotherapy, achieving weight loss magnitudes previously attainable only through bariatric surgery. This comparative analysis establishes tirzepatide as the most efficacious agent (15.0–20.9% weight loss), followed by semaglutide (14.9–16.0%), and liraglutide (~8%). All three agents demonstrate favorable cardiovascular safety profiles, with semaglutide and liraglutide showing established cardiovascular benefit in high-risk populations.

Gastrointestinal adverse events are common but generally mild-to-moderate and transient, with discontinuation rates of 4.3–7.0%. The choice among agents should be individualized based on patient phenotype, comorbidities, treatment goals, tolerability, and cost considerations. Tirzepatide offers maximal weight loss efficacy for patients requiring substantial weight reduction. Semaglutide provides robust weight loss with proven cardiovascular benefit. Liraglutide represents an established option with long-term safety data.

Future research priorities include head-to-head comparative effectiveness trials, long-term safety and durability studies, predictive biomarkers for treatment response, and cost-effectiveness analyses to inform access and reimbursement policies. As the obesity pharmacotherapy landscape continues to evolve with novel agents and combinations, GLP-1 receptor agonists will remain cornerstone therapies for patients with obesity and related cardiometabolic complications.
